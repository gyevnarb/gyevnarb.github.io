<b>I am on the job market for academic and industry research positions. You can read my [research statement](/blog/2026/science-of-automated-science/).</b>

AI systems for science have come a long way, but they still struggle to follow even basic principles of scientific integrity, such as "Don't p-hack" and "Check your dataset".
I study the **science of automated science:** how we can uphold scientific integrity by making automated discovery rigorous, correct, and accountable to people.
I combine computational models with empirical studies, working with [Atoosa Kasirzadeh](https://kasirzadeh.org/) and [Nihar Shah](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~nihars/) at the intersection of machine learning, cognitive science, and the philosophy of science.
I completed my PhD at the University of Edinburgh on explainable multi-agent behavior through counterfactual reasoning ([my thesis](https://era.ed.ac.uk/items/8d02a5aa-e2ae-4243-99b7-f639a3a14289)).

I am currently most curious about three directions:

- <b>How to diagnose epistemic risks of automated science:</b>
  [AI epistemic risks](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6873005) threaten to undermine the credibility of scientific evidence.
  We showed that [poisoning open datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10712) can steer AI research agents to fraudulent conclusions in half of all runs, and that open-ended [AI agents systematically deviate from observed human behavior](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06995) in large-scale proof formalization projects.
- <b>How to explain autonomous agents to support scientific understanding:</b>
  I develop and evaluate systems that generate explanations of the behavior of autonomous agents, such as the algorithm [MAAX (Multi-Agent Action eXplanation)](https://era.ed.ac.uk/items/8d02a5aa-e2ae-4243-99b7-f639a3a14289).
  Drawing on cognitive science, I test my designs with [relevant human stakeholders](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706598.3713509).
- <b>How to design and evaluate institutional processes that support accountability:</b>
  We built [greCAPTCHA](https://grecaptcha.com) to test whether authors understand their own papers, showing that it achieves an AUC of 0.9.
  I also mapped how research communities prioritize AI risks in [AI Safety for Everyone](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01020-y) and [Bridging the Gap in the Responsible AI Divides](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.14495).
